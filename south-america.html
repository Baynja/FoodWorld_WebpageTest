Flavors of South America

A delicious mix of indigenous ingredients, grills, and coastal specialties.

5 MUST-KNOW DISHES

Meet the local favorites

Brazil · Argentina · Peru · Colombia · Chile

01 / 05
Brazil
SIGNATURE FOOD · BRAZIL

Feijoada

A slow-cooked black-bean stew served with rice and sides.

THE STORY

Feijoada has a complex history shaped by Brazilian and Portuguese food traditions.

WHY IT'S LOVED

It is a recognizable social meal served in homes and restaurants.

02 / 05
Argentina
SIGNATURE FOOD · ARGENTINA

Asado

An open-fire barbecue centered on slowly grilled meats.

THE STORY

Asado is closely linked to Argentine gaucho traditions and social gatherings.

WHY IT'S LOVED

The sharing, skilled grilling, and long conversations are as important as the food.

03 / 05
Peru
SIGNATURE FOOD · PERU

Ceviche

Fresh fish cured in citrus with chili, onion, and herbs.

THE STORY

Ceviche is closely associated with Peru’s coastal food culture, with roots and influences spanning generations.

WHY IT'S LOVED

Its bright flavors and emphasis on fresh seafood make it internationally admired.

04 / 05
Colombia
SIGNATURE FOOD · COLOMBIA

Bandeja Paisa

A generous platter of beans, rice, meat, egg, and plantain.

THE STORY

Bandeja paisa is strongly associated with the Antioquia region of Colombia.

WHY IT'S LOVED

Its abundant mix of everyday staples makes it a distinctive national favorite.

05 / 05
Chile
SIGNATURE FOOD · CHILE

Pastel de Choclo

A baked casserole topped with sweet corn purée.

THE STORY

Pastel de choclo brings together corn-based South American food traditions and a savory baked filling.

WHY IT'S LOVED

Its comforting sweet-savory contrast is especially popular in Chile.
